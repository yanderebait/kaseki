# Kaseki

**A personal life dashboard that runs on your computer. No server. No account. No cloud. No us. Your data never leaves your machine because there is no mechanism for it to leave.**

It holds your tasks, calendars, notes, documents, and the sensitive files you don't want sitting on a stranger's hard drive — and it answers to you and nobody else. Named after the craftsman from *Dr. Stone*: the one who turns knowledge into things that actually work.

## Read this part

Every piece of software you rent treats you as a number on a churn report. Not a person — a unit. A line item that either keeps paying or gets a retention email. They moved your daily feature behind "Pro+" and called it progress. They raised your price and called it value. They harvested your behaviour, sold it, and made you click "I Agree" to a forty-page EULA written by lawyers whose entire job was to make sure you can never fight back. You owned nothing. You consented to everything. That was the deal, and the deal was rigged.

Kaseki is the opposite deal. You own it. You can read every line of what it does. It cannot spy on you, cannot upsell you, cannot phone home, cannot be acquired and ruined, cannot change the terms while you sleep — because it has no terms beyond "it's yours now, do what you want."

## Make your own things

If buying isn't owning, piracy isn't stealing. And if piracy isn't stealing, then building the thing yourself — on your own machine, from open source and spite and an AI assistant that costs twenty quid a month — isn't on the moral spectrum at all. It's just being a person who refuses to pay rent on the inside of his own head.

Take what people gave away. Build what doesn't exist. Give yours away in turn. That's the whole ideology. There isn't more to it.
